# DevBot Business Plan

## Executive Summary

DevBot is the governed engineering teammate for teams that want faster request-to-PR execution without losing review discipline.

The business model is straightforward:

- `DevBot` is the public front door
- `DEBO` is the upmarket workstation and governance expansion
- monetization starts with team productivity and expands into operator visibility, trust, approvals, and broader managed execution

## Problem

Engineering teams lose time to:

- repeated context switching across chat, repos, and review tools
- weak handoffs between request, execution, and approval
- AI usage that speeds up drafting but makes governance worse
- corrections that die in chat instead of becoming durable operating knowledge

## Solution

DevBot creates one governed execution lane:

1. request enters through chat or the connected workflow
2. task is scoped into a reviewable journey
3. execution produces code changes and review-ready output
4. high-risk actions preserve human approval
5. reviewer corrections can be taught instead of repeated manually

## Commercial Model

### Primary Buyers

- platform and engineering leads
- engineering managers
- technical reviewers and security owners
- agencies or small internal platform teams

### Monetization Schema

- `Free / sandbox`
  - limited guided requests
  - learning and evaluation
- `Team`
  - request-to-PR workflow
  - basic journey history
  - approval and reviewer controls
- `Business`
  - expanded concurrency
  - trust and memory controls
  - stronger reporting and operator visibility
- `Enterprise`
  - deployment governance
  - custom policy controls
  - procurement and compliance support
  - expansion into DEBO workstation and managed control surfaces

## Profitability Logic

DevBot should be profitable because the operating loop is narrow and defensible:

- acquisition is driven by a concrete productivity promise
- onboarding is tied to a visible first win: a reviewed shipped task
- expansion happens when teams need broader governance, not just more generation
- DEBO raises account value without changing the underlying journey architecture

Key profitability levers:

- keep the teammate scope tight
- avoid expensive generic assistant behavior
- use memory and approvals to reduce repeated reviewer effort
- convert teams that outgrow the teammate surface into DEBO workstation accounts

## North Star

`Reviewed request-to-PR completion rate`

Supporting metrics:

- median time from request to review-ready output
- reviewer acceptance rate
- reject-plus-teach reuse rate
- weekly active workspaces
- DEBO expansion conversion rate

## Go-To-Market

### Phase 1

- target Tolani ecosystem teams and operator-heavy internal workflows
- prove the request-to-PR loop
- collect review-drag and time-saved evidence

### Phase 2

- launch as the engineering teammate
- sell governance-backed execution instead of assistant novelty
- show the DEBO expansion path clearly

### Phase 3

- expand into DEBO where buyers need workstation-level visibility, trust posture, and approvals across multiple lanes

## Stakeholder Summary

DevBot is not the full platform story. It is the efficient wedge.

It wins when:

- one team can buy it quickly
- one workflow improves immediately
- one reviewer feels less drag
- and one account can expand cleanly into DEBO without replacing the architecture
